Research

Making AI work for the people who need it most

My research connects engineering, artificial intelligence, and experimental biology. At Tsinghua University and The Chinese University of Hong Kong, I worked on instrumentation, microfluidic systems, and AI-enabled biological measurements. At UCLA, I bring that engineering perspective to a new question: how can we find compounds that reduce pathological tau in Alzheimer's disease? Working with the Peng and Wang laboratories, I developed TauSeer and co-developed TauDiff and SSR-TauDiff. Our approach links molecular prediction and generative design to neuronal testing, using experimental results to refine the next round of predictions.

From predictions to biological evidence. TauSeer screened 53 million purchasable compounds. In the final prospective cohort, 22 of 95 evaluable candidates reduced seeded tau pathology by more than 30%, including two with reductions above 50%. These are preliminary laboratory findings, not demonstrated benefits in patients. My WHAM-funded project now adds female- and male-derived neuronal testing to inform which candidates advance.
